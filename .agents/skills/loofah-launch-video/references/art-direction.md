# Distinctive design and motion

The desired quality is an authored short film with the care of an independent animation studio. This is a creative standard, not a request to copy another studio's recognizable work. Keep the particular launch useful and clear; artistry should strengthen its meaning.

## Start with a visual idea

Name the idea in one sentence before coding scenes. For Loofah, useful raw material includes conversations becoming readable notes, a new model finding its place among existing models, and files remaining in the user's hands. Choose one recurring form that can evolve through that story: a ribbon, sheet, stack, wave, or window. A specific recurring form gives transitions a purpose and gives the film a memory.

Resolve a few key compositions as style frames: the opening, the central transformation/payoff, and the closing. Each should have a clear silhouette and typographic hierarchy, and be compelling as a still. Use them to discover the visual system rather than mechanically illustrating each sentence with another rounded card. Select the direction autonomously within the brief; this step is not a mandatory user approval gate.

The current forest palette, Fraunces, and canonical Loofah mark are identity anchors. They do not require repeating the homepage layout or keeping the same leaf photograph behind every shot. Mix a dark forest stage with cream paper compositions, botanical framing, or precise linework when the concept benefits. The illustrative treatment should feel specific to Loofah rather than a generic SaaS promo.

## Make scenes belong to one film

Design the outgoing and incoming poses together. Let a shape, line, word, or point of focus continue across the boundary. A waveform can straighten into a baseline; that line can become a document rule; the sheet can join a local file stack. These are visual metaphors, not claims about real app interactions.

Use transitions such as a traveling mask, a shared object changing scale/orientation, a camera following one form, or a cut matched on position and motion. Choose what the story needs. Avoid resetting to an empty backdrop before every new heading fades in. A useful transition tells the viewer how the next idea relates to the previous one.

Give the film a small number of memorable moments: perhaps an assertive title reveal, a satisfying transformation, and a quiet ownership payoff. Develop the best moments fully instead of scattering effects across every second. Keep enough context for the viewer to understand the transition's destination.

## Animate with intention and character

Think in poses and timing. Establish anticipation, a clear main action, and a precise settle. Use different weight and follow-through for a sheet of paper, a typographic line, and a solid model tile. Small overshoot or rotation can add personality when motivated; identical spring presets everywhere flatten that personality.

Build arcs and purposeful trajectories. Offset a supporting action slightly behind the main action, allowing the eye to follow cause and effect. A carefully controlled secondary movement can keep a hold alive, but everything need not float continuously. Use ease curves deliberately and reserve fast acceleration for emphasis. Check a motion proof at normal speed: a beautiful intermediate frame alone does not prove good animation.

Let rhythmic bursts alternate with composure. Arrivals can be energetic while text holds remain stable. Choose music phrase changes or a few accents to support major reveals; avoid a cut or bounce on every beat. Do not shrink reading time merely to make the piece feel busy.

## Give typography a performance

Use the word's meaning and hierarchy to choose its motion. Fraunces can arrive through a traveling mask, expand from a tightly composed block, or share a baseline with the next thought. Prefer whole words or short phrases that remain readable; endless letter-by-letter assembly is a poor default.

Plan line breaks at final size. Check actual fonts, italic overhang, spaces around version numbers and **ASR**, and the text during its reveal as well as at rest. Reserve strong scale contrast and an accent color for what matters. Keep secondary labels quiet enough to serve the composition without becoming unreadable.

## Build convincing illustration and space

Use original SVG, geometry, or code-native illustration when precise shapes and repeatability serve the concept. Apply one consistent perspective, line weight, corner language, shadow direction, and material logic. A small amount of dimensional layering or paper overlap can give a scene tactile presence without requiring a heavy 3D pipeline.

Texture should be subtle and intentional. If using grain or incidental variation, keep it deterministic and inspect it at export size so it does not turn into compression noise. Preserve canonical logo proportions; animate the supplied mark as an object rather than redrawing or deforming it into a different logo. Clearly distinguish conceptual illustration from captured UI. A local-file metaphor must not imply unsupported encryption or other new features.

## Compose sound with picture

Plan music and any sound accents during the animatic. The soundtrack should give the visual idea momentum and a deliberate finish. Consider a few quiet, licensed or original accents for a transformation or a landing when they improve the film. Avoid a catalogue of whooshes. Retain the chosen music's character and headroom; loudness is not a substitute for motion craft. Follow [music.md](music.md) for permissions, mixing, and verification.

## Review as a director

Compare the new version with the previous one. Can the viewer name the announcement immediately? Does a recognizable form connect the film? Are the strongest moments distinctive, with clear focus and satisfying transitions? Does the motion vary in weight and rhythm? Can every claim and ending line still be read? A request for stronger art direction should produce observable changes in these qualities.

Inspect the opening, transitions at several intermediate frames, settled key poses, and the last frame. Watch short motion proofs at normal speed when the environment permits. Fix accidental clipping, strange pivots, collisions, repeated blank resets, and awkward easing before a full render. Keep the final Loofah identity and local ownership message on screen long enough to register.
